# Independent review: identity v2 specs

- **Date:** 2026-10-29
- **Reviewer:** independent; did not write the specs.
- **Scope:** every file in `docs/design/specs/identity-v2/` (README, identity-model, enrollment-v2, signatures-and-encoding, grants-v2, room-log-v2, mls-integration, keys-rotation-recovery, migration, `vectors/gen-vectors.mjs`, `vectors/vectors.json`), checked against `docs/security/hardening-plan.md` (A1 to A8, both decision rounds), `docs/security/protocol-review.md` and section 8 of the Rooms architecture document (design research, not published).
- **Method:** read-only design review, plus running the vector generator and an independent recomputation of the vectors with `node:crypto`. No spec was edited. Nothing touched `~/.paseo`, a daemon or the network.

## 1. Verdict

**Ready with fixes.** The design closes most of A1 to A8 and is a large improvement over M1. The structure is sound: admin key separate from host key, per-room device keys, mutual Noise IK with the room bound into the prologue, domain-separated signatures with working vectors, a grant allowlist, enforce-then-persist revocation, and roster equality for MLS.

Three high-severity issues should be fixed in the specs before code, because two of them change formats:

1. **S-1:** statements are not bound to a log position, so a host can re-sequence old admin statements (role, lane access, policy, `admin.added`). This contradicts the central A2 claim that the host cannot change membership.
2. **S-2:** under the default `same-person` policy, a stolen device can add devices without a prompt and can also remove or rekey the person's other devices. A thief can take over the membership and lock out the real person.
3. **S-3:** enrollment resume does not pin the SAS inputs. Unless the original `h`, `Nm` and `Nh` are pinned, the grinding attack that section 6.1 itself describes comes back after a reconnect.

A narrow re-review of the changed sections is enough after the fixes. A full second review is not needed.

## 3. Findings

Severity reflects the impact if implemented as written. Everything below is judgement from reading the specs unless it is marked as verified in section 2.

| S-1 | **high** | room-log-v2 2, 5; identity-model 4.1 | A `Statement.body` has `roomId`, `ts` (display only) and `data`, but nothing that ties it to a position or a prior state. `apply` has no duplicate or replay rule. A host, or a host-key thief with the log, can append an old, validly signed admin statement again. Examples: `member.role.changed {bob, admin}` after Bob was demoted; an older `room.policy.changed` that restores `deviceAddApproval: "same-person"` (only `minVersion` is monotonic); `member.lanes.changed` that restores removed lane access; `admin.added` for an admin key that was later removed (nothing puts removed admin keys on a denylist). Members fold the result and roster equality then follows the replayed roster. This defeats "a host key can only issue grants … cannot add a device" for roles, lanes, policy and admins. Device keys are protected by the denylist. | Add `base: {seq, hash}` (the head the producer saw) to every `Statement.body`. Require `base.seq < entry.seq`, and require that no statement with the same `bodyHash` was already applied. For state-setting types, also include the previous value (`prevRole`, `prevLanes`, `prevPolicyHash`) and reject the statement if it does not match the current state. Denylist removed and revoked admin keys. Add vectors and a fold test for a replayed statement. |
| S-2 | **high** | identity-model 6.2, 6.3; room-log-v2 4.3; keys-rotation-recovery 4.1, 6.3 | With `deviceAddApproval = "same-person"` (the default), anyone holding a copy of device A's keys can do all of the following: add device B (the "SAS on two screens" is the thief's own two screens), auto-approved; issue `device.removed` for the person's other devices (allowed for "D of the same member"); and `device.rekeyed` A itself (also auto-approved), which puts the real A's keys on the denylist. The person ends with no device and must go through the admin. The thief keeps B, which 6.3 removes only if someone thinks to look for `addedBy`. The room notice ("Sam added a device") is the only signal, and the real person may never see it once their devices are gone. | (1) A device statement may remove **only its own device**; removing another device needs an admin. (2) Auto-approved adds take effect after a hold (for example 24 h). During the hold every other device of the member gets a notice with a one-click veto (`device.add.vetoed`, which removes the requester as compromised). (3) A device younger than the hold period cannot auto-add. (4) Auto-approve rekeys only when no other device of the member objects within the hold. (5) Removing a device with `reason: "compromise"` automatically offers to cascade to devices it added. This keeps decision R2-2 (auto-approval with notice) and adds an abuse brake. Ask the user (Q1). |
| S-3 | **high** | enrollment-v2 5.6 (cited, but no such section), 7.3, 9 | The member journal says that in state `pending` it sends `enroll.resume` and shows the SAS again ("SAS inputs stored"), and that the host "replays the latest host message". `h` is the SAS salt, and every reconnect has a new handshake and therefore a new `h`. The spec does not say that the SAS stays bound to the **original** `h`, `Nm` and `Nh`, or that the member must refuse a resumed `enroll.pending` with a different `Nh` or `pendingId` after it has revealed `Nm`. If an implementation recomputes with the new `h`, or accepts a new `Nh`, a man in the middle who already knows `Nm` can grind its ephemeral or `Nh` to match the host's SAS. That is the attack section 6.1 says the commitment prevents. Section 5.6, which section 4 cites for `enroll.resume`, does not exist. | Write section 5.6: the SAS is computed once, from the first session's `h`, and is stored sealed together with `Nm`, `Nh` and `pendingId`. On resume the member accepts only a byte-identical `enroll.pending`, and never re-reveals into a different pending. The host, likewise, never issues a new `Nh` for an existing pending. If any input would change, abort and burn. Add a test that a resumed session with a different `Nh` aborts. |
| S-4 | medium | enrollment-v2 5.4, 6.1, 7.1 | SAS attempts are not really limited. A man in the middle holding the token can open a pending enrollment, receive `Nh`, compute the host-side SAS (it already knows its own `Nm'`), and abort with `cancelled` or disconnect if the SAS does not match the member-side value. That returns the invite to `open` and counts as neither a token failure nor a SAS rejection. Only the 10/min endpoint bucket limits the retries. That is about 300 tries over a 30-minute invite (success about 3×10⁻⁴) and about 14 000 over a 24 h invite (about 1.4 %). The README and decision log claim "very few attempts". | Count every pending enrollment that reached `enroll.pending` (that is, disclosed `Nh`) against the invite. Burn the invite after 2 or 3, and log `invite.burned {reason:"attempts"}`. Show cancelled attempts on the admin's join card. |
| S-5 | medium | room-log-v2 4.2, 7; enrollment-v2 5.3 step 6, 5.4 | `invite.created`, `enrollment.pending`, `invite.contested` (and `device.link.created` with `linkId`) put the invite id in the plaintext log. That log is served to every active device and visible to the host. Step 6 burns an invite after 3 bad MACs naming its id. So any member can burn every open invite and device link, which is P-15 again with members as the attacker. Section 5.4 says "invite ids appear only inside invite codes", which is not true. | Log `inviteRef = H("invite-ref", inviteId)` instead, or derive `inviteId` from the token (`H("invite-id", token)`). Alternatively, count MAC failures against the invite only when the requester is not an active roster device, and rate-limit the rest per static key. Fix the 5.4 sentence and the README residual-risk row. |
| S-6 | medium | mls-integration 4.2, 4.3 | A commit names its own reference roster `{seq, hash}`, and the checks only require the leaf set to equal *that* roster. Nothing requires it to be the **current** roster at the position where the host sequences the commit. A member can therefore commit against an older roster: an Update that keeps a pending-removed leaf, or an Add authorised by a role the committer has since lost. The denylist blocks re-adding removed device keys, but not stale role authority or the undoing of a pending removal. | Require `roster.seq` to equal the last roster-changing entry before the commit's own log entry, and reject otherwise. Authorise committer role from that same roster. |
| S-7 | medium | room-log-v2 6.3, 6.6 | A member raises `fork` "either way" when a peer's claimed head disagrees with its own, even if the host returns the member's own version. The claimed head travels unsigned in MLS AAD. One malicious member can therefore make every honest member stop sending in the room (6.6), with no host misbehaviour at all. | Raise `fork` only with an equivocation proof (two `hsig`-valid entries at one `seq`). An unproven disagreement becomes `unverified_head_claim`, attributed to the sender's credential, and stops nothing. |
| S-8 | medium | keys-rotation-recovery 3, 4.3, 6.6; room-log-v2 4.1, 5 | Admin compromise recovery has unclear precedence and races. (a) With `adminThreshold: 1`, a stolen admin key can `admin.key.revoked` the honest admin with a backdated `revokedAt` before the honest admin revokes it. Whichever entry the host sequences first wins, and the loser's statements turn "suspect". (b) `revokedAt` is a time, but the fold compares it to a "stmt position", which mixes units. (c) The fold applies suspect statements and only marks them, so a ghost device added by a suspect statement stays in the roster, and roster equality then *requires* its leaf until an admin reverts it. (d) `room.closed` from a stolen admin key is final, and the recovery key cannot undo it. | Let recovery-key statements take precedence: `admin.key.revoked` by R is accepted even if the target admin revoked others first, and it voids those revocations. Bound `revokedAt` to no earlier than the revoker's own `validFrom` and the target's `validFrom`. Define the fold to exclude suspect roster changes from the reference roster until `admin.reaffirmed`. Make `room.closed` reversible by R within a window, or require R or a second admin. Express every revocation cut-off as a log position. |
| S-9 | medium | grants-v2 4 step 5, 7.1 | Role downgrades are not in the list of access-reducing operations, and the lane endpoint does not compare `claims.role` with the current roster role. A grant minted for `steer` keeps working for up to 12 h after a demotion to `watch`. This is harmless while lanes are watch-only and matters from Phase 2. | Add `member.role.changed` (downward) to 7.1, so that `current[(device, lane)]` is cleared and a reissue is pushed. In step 5, require `claims.role == roster role`. |
| S-10 | medium | enrollment-v2 3.2, 3.5 | A replayed IK message 1 lets the responder finish its side of the handshake with the real device static. Section 3.5 says a second "successful handshake" from the same static closes the live session with `superseded`. Anyone who recorded message 1, including the relay, can therefore repeatedly kick a device off. | Count a session as established, and supersede the older one, only after the first valid transport message from the initiator (key confirmation). Keep the older session until then. |
| S-11 | medium | keys-rotation-recovery 4.1 step 6, 5; mls-integration 4.3 | Device rekey says that the device itself commits "Remove old leaf + Add new leaf" and that its new leaf "joins by Welcome". ## 6. Open questions for the user

1. **Device auto-approval brakes (S-2).** Do you accept a hold period (for example 24 h) with a veto from the person's other devices, and a rule that a device may remove only itself? This keeps "auto-approved with notice" but stops a stolen device from taking over.
2. **Who may reveal project names (S-16)?** Only the lane owner for their own lanes, or any admin room-wide?
3. **Grant TTL (S-17).** Keep the per-invite range of 1 to 24 h, or fix it at 12 h?
4. **SAS attempt cap (S-4).** Burn an invite after 2 or 3 abandoned join attempts? The cost: a member whose network drops mid-join may need a new invite.
5. **Room closure (S-8).** Should `room.closed` need the recovery key or a second admin, or be reversible for a short window?
